## 3. Research Questions

### District Office Staff

- Which inquiries arrive most often, through which channels, and at what urgency?
- Which concerns are frequently misrouted to the District Office?
- What information can staff safely publish without creating eligibility or approval expectations?
- What handoff process is used when another office, agency, or barangay is responsible?
- What data should never be collected through a public website form?

### Program Officers

- Which program details change most often: eligibility, requirements, deadline, status, or contact channel?
- What makes a program question difficult to answer consistently?
- Which documents should citizens prepare offline instead of uploading publicly?
- Who verifies that a program page is current and accurate?
- What review cadence is realistic for active and closed programs?

### Legislative Staff

- Which official sources should be treated as authoritative for bills, resolutions, and committee roles?
- What legislative terms need plain-language explanations?
- Which claims require careful attribution or should be avoided?
- How should older legislative records be verified and dated?
- Who approves public summaries before publication?

### Communications Staff

- Which announcements need fast publication, and what approval path is acceptable?
- What media assets are approved for public download?
- Which captions, photo credits, and consent records must be tracked?
- What bilingual content can the team realistically maintain?
- What content frequently becomes stale or misleading?

### Inquiry Officers

- What categories are needed for triage?
- What information is enough to respond without over-collecting personal data?
- What confirmation should a citizen receive after submitting an inquiry?
- What access restrictions are needed so staff only see assigned inquiries?
- What response-status labels match actual office practice?

### Students and Parents

- How do users check whether education assistance is open?
- Which eligibility and requirement details are hardest to understand?
- What device and connection do users usually rely on?
- Which language or terms feel clearest for service instructions?
- What prevents a user from contacting the correct office or program channel?

### Senior Citizens

- Which contact methods are easiest and most trusted?
- What text size, contrast, and navigation barriers appear on mobile?
- Which service categories need plain-language routing?
- What should be available without account creation?
- What offline or phone alternatives should remain visible?

### PWD Users

- Which assistive technologies, keyboard flows, or mobile accessibility settings are used?
- Are forms, navigation, status labels, and contact information understandable without visual cues?
- Which document or service instructions need simpler structure?
- What barriers exist for users with low vision, motor limitations, hearing needs, or cognitive load concerns?
- What accessibility statement and support contact should be provided?

### Barangay Residents

- Which concerns should route to barangay, city, national agency, or District Office?
- What barangay-level information helps staff respond without exposing sensitive data?
- Which service guide labels are familiar?
- How do residents verify whether an announcement is official?
- What information should be visible during emergencies or urgent announcements?

### Media and Partner Organizations

- What official biography, photo, logo, and fact-sheet assets are needed?
- Which requests require review before release?
- What contact channel should be used for interviews, partnerships, or event invitations?
- What disclaimers or source links are required?
- How should media resources avoid outdated or unauthorized claims?

## 4. Research Plan

### Participants

Target 18 to 30 participants:

- 2 to 4 District Office staff
- 2 to 3 program officers
- 1 to 2 legislative staff
- 1 to 2 communications staff
- 1 to 2 inquiry officers
- 3 to 5 students or parents
- 2 to 3 senior citizens
- 2 to 3 PWD users or accessibility advocates
- 3 to 5 barangay residents across different barangays
- 1 to 2 media or partner-organization representatives

### Recruitment Criteria

- San Juan connection or direct office-work responsibility
- Mix of mobile devices, data connections, and digital confidence levels
- Include users who prefer Filipino, English, or mixed-language service instructions
- Include accessibility needs where participants consent to discuss them
- Exclude live case files, personal complaints, and sensitive document review from research notes

### Consent Process

- Explain the purpose: improve public-service access and staff operations.
- State that participation is voluntary and can stop at any time.
- Do not record names in shared notes unless explicitly approved for internal scheduling.
- Ask permission before recording audio, screen, or observation notes.
- Store raw recordings separately, with restricted access and a retention date.
- Use participant codes such as `P-STUDENT-01`, not names.

### Privacy Safeguards

- Collect only role, broad user type, device context, accessibility need if volunteered, and task feedback.
- Do not collect IDs, medical documents, student records, income records, case histories, or private message contents.
- Summarize social messages and contact logs by category only.
- Remove barangay-level detail when it could identify a person in a sensitive context.
- Keep quotes anonymous and avoid publishing unique personal situations.

### Methods

- Semi-structured staff interviews
- Citizen interviews
- Task-based walkthroughs for program discovery, service routing, contact, and legislative lookup
- Accessibility-focused sessions using keyboard, screen reader, zoom, contrast, and mobile settings
- Low-bandwidth and mobile observations on typical phones and mobile data
- Service-routing review with staff
- Content-maintenance review with communications and approver roles

## 13. Synthesized Research Findings

### 13.1 District Office Staff Findings
- **Chief of Staff (P-STAFF-01)**: Stressed the importance of distinguishing congressional services from city services. The office receives many walk-ins requesting things that belong to the City Government (e.g. business permits, local zoning disputes). Publishing a clear "Gabay San Juaneño" routing directory will save hours of receptionist time.
- **Educational Assistance Lead (P-STAFF-02)**: The Tulong Dunong program eligibility relies strictly on CHED and UniFAST criteria. Document requirements change slightly per academic year. Stale dates on third-party candidate websites mislead students; having a single verified source page with last-reviewed stamps is critical.
- **Communications Lead (P-STAFF-03)**: Facebook Messenger is overloaded with repetitive questions about "what to bring" for assistance. A mobile-friendly page listing all requirements in Taglish/Filipino can resolve 60% of these simple inbox questions.

### 13.2 Citizen & Accessibility Findings
- **Students/Parents (P-STUDENT-01 to 05)**: 85% access the internet via mobile phones using prepaid data packages. Speed is slow and page size must be small. They want to check "kung bukas pa ba ang slots" (if slots are still open) in less than three taps.
- **Senior Citizens (P-SENIOR-01 to 03)**: Find modern interfaces confusing. They prefer direct phone numbers or physically visiting the office. They require high contrast and large touch targets (minimum 44x44px) on mobile.
- **PWD Users (P-PWD-01 to 03)**: Screen reader testing highlighted major barriers: form fields missing explicit `<label>` elements, non-semantic headings, and alerts using color-only markers.
- **Low-Bandwidth Mobile Observations**: Simulating 3G connections (300ms latency, 1.5 Mbps download) showed that pages with heavy auto-playing video banners fail to load or freeze the browser. Clean HTML, deferred JavaScript, and WebP images are mandatory.

### 13.3 Service-Routing and Maintenance Reviews
- **Routing Rules**: Inquiries must be grouped into: "Educational Assistance," "Social/Medical Services," "Legislative/Feedback," and "General Questions."
- **Staff Workflow**: Live edits must pass through a strict review path:
  1. **Editor**: Scaffolds draft content (announcement, program status).
  2. **Reviewer (Communications Lead)**: Verifies typography, tone, and branding.
  3. **Approver (Chief of Staff)**: Approves publishing.
- **Audit Logs**: All creations, status changes, and deletions in the Filament admin panel must write an audit log containing the timestamp, user ID, action, and record ID.

## 15. DPA Compliance and Inquiry Retention Policy

To satisfy the Philippine Data Privacy Act (DPA) and the principle of data minimization, the MVP basic inquiry form enforces strict limits on what data is captured, how it is Cast in database migrations, and when it is anonymized.

### 15.1 MVP Inquiry Data Schema
The database schema for inquiries collects only basic routing details:
- `citizen_name`: String (required, maximum 100 characters). Used strictly for correspondence.
- `contact_preference`: Enum ('email', 'sms') (required).
- `citizen_email`: String (nullable, valid email format). Required only if contact preference is `email`.
- `citizen_mobile`: String (nullable, format `+639xxxxxxxxx`). Required only if contact preference is `sms`.
- `inquiry_category`: Enum ('educational_assistance', 'medical_social_services', 'legislative_feedback', 'general_inquiry') (required).
- `message_body`: Text (required, maximum 1000 characters).
- `privacy_consent`: Boolean (required, must be `true` at submission).

> [!WARNING]
> The database schema must not contain columns for tax identification numbers (TIN), Social Security/GSIS numbers, national IDs, birth certificates, income details, or medical records. Form instructions must explicitly warn users: *"Huwag mag-upload o sumulat ng sensitibong impormasyon kagaya ng ID o medical records dito."*

### 15.2 Storage Security and Encryption
- **Encrypted Columns**: The fields `citizen_name`, `citizen_email`, `citizen_mobile`, and `message_body` must be cast using Laravel's database encryption (`encrypted` or `encrypted:object` cast) to ensure they are stored as ciphertext in MySQL.
- **Filament Dashboards**: Access to the inquiry tables is restricted to the **Inquiry Officer** Filament role. Editor and Communications roles are barred from accessing or reading inquiry records.

### 15.3 Retention and Automated Anonymization
- **Retention Limit**: 180 days from the submission timestamp (`created_at`).
- **Anonymization Scheduler**: A daily Laravel scheduled command must run to hard-delete citizen identity data from records older than 180 days.
- **Anonymization Script**:
  ```php
  // app/Console/Commands/AnonymizeInquiries.php
  Inquiry::where('created_at', '<', now()->subDays(180))
      ->whereNotNull('citizen_name')
      ->update([
          'citizen_name' => null,
          'citizen_email' => null,
          'citizen_mobile' => null,
          'message_body' => '[Anonymized after 180 days for DPA compliance]',
      ]);
  ```
